## Nine Lights for Bear: A November Tea Party by Amber Candlelight

In November, evenings come early to the Home, bringing with them the soft patter of rain outside the window and that special, thick silence in which the old wooden beams under the ceiling creak quietly as they settle down to sleep.

When the people finally go off to their rooms and the overhead light goes out, a different time begins on the Oriental rug in the living room.

In a cozy miniature suite of furniture, set out right in the middle of the room before the fireplace, the whole old guard had gathered. On the little sofa with the carved back, **23-year-old Bear from Mothercare** had settled in with dignity — the senior resident, who remembers this Home better than anyone. Beside him, sprawled royally in an armchair and clutching her treasured ice-cream cone, sat **Dolce the monkey** — the Steampunk Queen of the card table, who for one evening had traded her cards for polite conversation. And opposite them, his soft plush ears drooping, sat faithful **[Butler](/stories/butler-a-retriever-from-an-ancient-line-of-guardians/)**, the retriever — the keeper of silence and of the night's peace.

On the pouffe-table before them steamed three vintage black coffee cups and saucers with a delicate floral pattern; on an antique silver dessert stand sat little berry cakes; and on a rough wooden stump a little way off, a round-bellied white teapot was keeping warm.

"It's a bit damp in the fireplace room tonight," Bear grumbled quietly, adjusting his plush paw. "The logs may be crackling in the fire, but the rain is drumming on the window as if it wants to get right through to our table. What's missing is that enveloping kind of warmth."

"Then let's ask our tall new neighbor," Queen Dolce smiled slyly, shifting her grip on her ice cream, and nodded toward the stairs.

There, like a silver tree from an old English fairy tale, stood the **Silver Giant, the girandole**. Not long ago he had stood gloomy and dark in a corner, wrapped in a century of patina; but now, washed clean of the ages and sealed with warm wax, he held nine "guttering" candles on his nine Baroque branches.

The Giant gave a soft chime of a silver acanthus leaf, as if clearing his throat before an important speech, and lit his amber flames one by one — not at full, ceremonial strength, but in a special midnight mode:

* **The first little flame** sparked for the old *Bell* harmonium from Ontario — so that its keys wouldn't miss the music on a rainy night.
* **The second** gently touched the carved mirror above the fireplace, in whose reflection the Looking-Glass World lies dozing.
* **The third** fell as a warm golden glint on the Chesterfield leather of the armchairs, which keep the traces of cats' dreams.
* **The fourth and fifth** warmed the patterns of the Oriental rug, turning the woolen threads into a quiet forest glade.
* **The sixth** winked at the Art Deco wall sconces — the elder relatives from whom the Giant's "wax" drips were painted.
* **The seventh** warmed Butler's soft ears, and he gave a deep, contented sigh, glancing at his coffee cup.
* **The eighth** settled like a golden crown on Dolce the monkey's shoulders, making her ice cream seem sweeter still.
* And **the ninth** — the warmest, curving like a living flame in the wind — stayed shining right on the silver dessert stand with the little cakes, for old Bear.

And just then, from behind the back of the little sofa, stepping soundlessly across the rugs, the Home's living guardian came to see what the light was about — a sphynx with eyes as curious as two pieces of amber. She sat down gracefully at the very edge of the tea table, looked the three friends over, checked that the cakes were in order, and purred her approval, as if taking this nighttime council under her protection.

The amber light of the nine candles spread through the room so thickly and softly that the rain outside stopped seeming cold at once — it turned into a quiet lullaby. Every drop of "guttering wax" on the Giant's silver branches breathed peace.

"Now this is nice," Bear whispered, closing his eyes. "Now it isn't just a house. It's Home."
