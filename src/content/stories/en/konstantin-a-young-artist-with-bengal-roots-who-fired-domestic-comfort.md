## Konstantin: A Young Artist with Bengal Roots Who Fired Domestic Comfort

### 😾 A Man's Wailing on the Utility Ledge

One day, the garden's harmonious quiet was shattered by a strange, inexplicable, deafening male wail. The unknown performer was loudly and dramatically informing the world of his hard fate and life's misfortunes. The Home's tailed female faction grew alarmed instantly, rallying around the one doctrine they all understood — find the enemy and defeat him.

Only Mum managed to hear the genuine despair inside those cries correctly, and set off to find the storyteller. The source of the drama turned out to be on the Home's utility ledge, right under the bathroom window. It was an enormous, young cat, his natural build still recognizable even through severe emaciation: a big head, powerful paws, and shoulder blades jutting sharply from thinness. It was misfortune in its purest form — the boy had simply had the bad luck of being born to people who never taught him to trust. The first natural instinct was to feed the newcomer. But the wild guest was terrified of humans and flinched from any outstretched hand, ready to dissolve into the shrubbery in an instant.

Diplomacy was called for: Mum began cautiously tossing dry kibble through the bathroom window, one piece at a time. The cat would warily gather up the food and leave. Soon these visits became a daily ritual: the scruffy drifter would arrive on the ledge, dutifully deliver his portion of complaints about life, and collect his well-earned fee.

### 🌙 A Pact on the Terrace and the Bengal's Return

The turning point came late one evening. The Home's silence was torn apart by Bibisechka's frightened yowl — the sphynx had discovered two enormous green eyes staring straight at her out of the darkness beyond the window glass. Mum went outside with a portion of food, and it was that very night that the cat first crossed his inner line of fear: he came up and ate carefully, right out of a human hand. The distance of trust had officially shrunk.

Soon the cat grew bold enough to change locations and came by in daylight, onto the front terrace. This time Mum greeted him not with dry rations but with real, fresh meat, quietly conducting a full visual audit along the way. The following day, a medical arsenal was prepared: parasite drops and healing medication. The street fighter proved remarkably cooperative — he allowed his fur to be treated and patiently let his injured paw be healed.

From then on, vitamins and supplements were regularly added to his diet. The results weren't long in coming: the paw healed, the coat grew glossy, revealing gorgeous Bengal genetics, and the cat himself gained weight, filled out, and turned into a powerful beauty with wise olive eyes. He stopped crying entirely — his face now showed only calm trust.

### 🏛️ A Philosophy of Independence

The cat quickly learned the dignified name he'd been given, Konstantin, and eagerly began answering to it. The script of his visits acquired a strict etiquette: he arrives on the terrace for his portion of good food, eats it all calmly and properly from his own personal bowl, silently expresses gratitude with a look, and leaves.

Mum would happily welcome Konstantin into the Home and the family (however categorically opposed the other furry girls might be to that union), but the boy chose full independence. He firmly rejected soft sofas, lace tablecloths, and domestic life. He used to linger for long stretches on the roof of one of the outbuildings after a hearty meal — now he's claimed the garden pergola instead. Konstantin sits regally on the wooden beams above and watches Alyonka with a faint Bengal-eyed squint whenever she starts noisily scolding someone below. He still has no intention of moving into the Home, preferring to remain free — the most devoted guardian of this world's outer borders. Mum is a little disappointed, but the laws of freedom in Covent Garden Syndrome are sacred for everyone.
