## Night Chronicles of the Home: Sierra Snows, the Olive Shore and the Guardians of the Entrance

When the ceiling chandeliers go dark in the Home and the footsteps fall quiet, the hour of things with a history begins. In the half-light of the hallway, where wrought-iron sconces cast warm glints on old silver reliquaries of the Immaculata, the senior Guardians of the Entrance keep their watch.

### 🏛️ The old guard of the hallway: Theo, Manuel and Chris

The first to arrive in this Home were two: **Theo**, an optimist from Murcia, and **Manuel**, a fastidious aesthete from Granada. For many years they lived in the secluded, sunlit big dining room — the Lady of the House's personal paradise, full of the shimmer of crystal, porcelain and old silver. They lived in the warmth of the radiator, on a wooden console beneath a gilded mirror — but naked, and broken by time. Beside them lay the wise old Grandfather Meerschaum Pipe, who taught them the first law of old things: *"Better to wait meekly for your finest hour, kept warm and respected for your age, than to keep breaking in ungrateful hands."*

Their finest hour came. Theo was given dense Parisian armor of French *Chanel* tweed, with a bougainvillea-colored silk handkerchief in the pocket, a shining brass crown, and a pair of bold neon skis that his brother from Granada had told him so much about. From a floating little chair in the corner of the hallway, he greets guests with the open, bright gaze of someone who expects no trickery.

Manuel, who in his day had seen the black-and-white checkerboard marble of old cathedrals and the living fire of wax candles, became a Milanese eccentric at the very top of the mirrored portal. He was wrapped in azure silk and the bright zigzags of *Missoni*, showered in gold, crowned with a "trembling" gilded crown and given fluffy white wings. And when Andalusia cooled down to +17 °C, his perfect little toes were warmed in mink slippers with daring carmine soles — one slipper is deliberately balanced on the very tip of his foot. Manuel scans newcomers more strictly than anyone: *"Why have you come? With what thoughts do you cross the threshold of Our Home?"*

Soon they were joined by swift **Chris** — an Eastern prince in glossy silk of explosive fuchsia, deep black velvet and large chunks of raw turquoise. He took his throne at the top of a carved sideboard before an antique mirror, leaving his feet proudly bare, in the company of a bronze greyhound and a lady in a crinoline.

### 👑 The legend of the House of de las Nieves: the snows of the Sierra Nevada

But this story turned out to have a sequel, with roots reaching back to the 16th century, to the windswept foothills of Granada's Sierra Nevada. That is where the ancient house of **de las Nieves** (*"of the Snows"*) began. Its founder was granted a coat of arms with a silver ermine on an azure field for leading a royal convoy across the snowbound passes in the depths of a bitter winter.

<figure>
  <img src="/photos/sierra-snows-the-olive-shore-and-the-guardians-of-the-entrance-12.jpg" alt="The coat of arms of the house of de las Nieves — a silver ermine on an azure field beneath a crown — above a stone fireplace with lit candles" />
</figure>

Unlike the dark-skinned southerners, the Nieves family passed down from generation to generation a rare Visigothic "mark of the blood": **piercing cornflower-blue eyes and golden-copper curls** that shone like red gold in the sun.

By the end of the 19th century the ancient house was on the verge of dying out: the last marquis had only one child, a daughter, the beautiful Doña Concepción. So that the great name, the castle and the entailed estate would not be lost, the royal chancery approved a special marriage contract when she wed a noble caballero from coastal Andalusia: **all children of this union would have the privilege of placing their mother's surname, Nieves, first**.

And so three heirs came into the world — of one blood, but with different fates and different statures:

* **Don Leo Nieves** — the firstborn, a solid and weighty keeper of traditions with a proud carriage of the head.

### 🌿 Cousin Gabriel from Málaga: dusty sage and drops of red wine

Beside the tall, snowy Nieves branch there always stood their coastal cousin on their father's side — light-eyed **Gabriel (Gabi)** from sunny Málaga. He grew up not among mountain passes but in the quiet of the coast's olive groves and citrus orchards (hence his future aristocratic outfit in dusty sage — the shade of old olive leaves under the southern sun).

In the turbulent 1930s, when Spain's old aristocratic houses were hastily being shut up, loyal servants, in darkness and confusion, hid the family treasures in heavy wooden chests in the attics of "grandmothers' houses." There the Nieves children and their cousin Gabriel spent decades. Under the weight of the things packed around them, the fragile plaster at their fingertips chipped, exposing the old metal armature beneath.

It was in just such a "grandmother's house" near the Málaga coast that Gabriel was found. And today he is already home, taking his first gentle baths on the restoration workbench. The bathing is not easy: under a layer of century-old dust, the magnificent warm, creamy tone of his original skin came through — but on his chest and tummy, only on the front, since for many years he lay face up, indelible greenish speckles appeared. Once, during some boisterous Andalusian feast or a hurried packing-up in the last century, someone spilled a few drops of red wine on him.

Those wine splashes never gave way to water, but no one is going to scrub them off by force: they have stayed on his skin as a living seal of Málaga history. Very soon his little fingers will take shape again, his massive, time-worn crown will shine with noble silver, and Gabi himself will be dressed in dusty-sage velvet, ready to welcome his snowy cousins of the house of Nieves to the Home.
