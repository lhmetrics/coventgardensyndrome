## Butler: A Retriever from an Ancient Line of Guardians

If Bear from Mothercare is the Patriarch and living chronicle of the Covent Garden Syndrome universe, then his Friend, the golden plush retriever Butler, is the very Soul of the peace here. A name that in English means the head of a household's staff became, in his plush passport, the highest of titles. For he comes from an ancient and noble line of Guardians. Butler is not just a dog — he is the chief steward of silence, comfort and impeccable order in the lavender zone of Covent Garden.

He came to the Home exactly when he was most needed: when the younger Son had just turned one. While the world around the little person whirled, bustled and built itself up, this cream-colored dog with a cozy ginger patch around his left eye chose for himself the quietest and most important service of all. In those years his main duty was to sit soundlessly on guard by the cradle, carefully filtering the rustles of the night and taking into his soft heart the first, most fragile childhood dreams. He keeps them there, safely, to this day.

This Home by the sea is already the third country in his and Bear's long plush biography. They are true gentleman-travelers, who know how roads smell, how flights feel from inside a suitcase, and how silence sounds in different corners of the planet. All this international experience has left Butler deeply cosmopolitan: putting on his round, scholarly spectacles, at night he likes to leaf through books in different languages — from old volumes of Dickens to Amor Towles's novel *A Gentleman in Moscow*, the story of how to keep one's dignity and create a whole universe of comfort within the bounds of a single space.

Butler is a true *observador* — a wise and deep observer. You will never catch him getting drawn into heated arguments on the top shelf of the *Bell* harmonium. Even when the eccentric Steampunk Queen Dolce starts rustling her cards too loudly, peeking out from her mirrored corner and egging everyone on to sweet household mischief, Butler only softly closes his eyes. He doesn't judge. He understands that the Queen of the card table is supposed to be eccentric. His wisdom lies in complete acceptance and unshakable support that needs no extra words.

Together with Bear, he has witnessed the greatest miracle and the greatest drama of this Home — the children growing up. The plush gentlemen watched the boys grow, fly off across distant oceans, come back filling the rooms with noisy stories, and leave again for their grown-up lives. They remember how deeply the parents worried at those moments of parting, and how the sons, step by step, gradually grew wiser and became real men.

On the hardest evenings, when Mom's heart froze with worry for her sons far away, Butler would settle at her feet with all his soft plush weight. By his very presence, by the warm squint of his left eye and his calm breathing, he filled her with a quiet, unshakable confidence. Butler is a wise dog, and he knows for certain: boys always grow wise a little later than girls. But by thirty they are sure to grow up for good. And Mom believes him, sincerely hoping so — even if sometimes a light, loving smile of doubt flickers in her eyes.

When deep night falls, the lavender zone turns into a private club. Butler and Bear hold their traditional tea party. They spread out a snow-white tablecloth embroidered with roses and bring out their favorite porcelain tea set. They drink nothing but Earl Grey — the real thing, fragrant with bergamot. It was Butler who once told Bear the old sea legend of a British ship caught in a storm, on board which precious bergamot oil spilled and soaked into the bales of tea leaves. Bear listened with bated breath, and Butler quietly whispered to the human: *"Why don't we plant a real bergamot tree in our garden, so that the Home's Diary keeps forever that delicate citrus scent of a bygone age?"*

Sometimes, when the night calls for a special mood and the moon peeks in through the tall window, the gentlemen take out tiny, fragile Murano glasses of deep blue and pour into each a single drop of fragrant liqueur from collectible bottles that hold the stories of faraway countries. They take a tiny, purely symbolic sip — breathing in the aroma of memories rather than drinking — nibble the sweets Mom brings them from all over the world, and muse on eternal things while gazing at old folios.

Both Bear and Butler know the main secret of this place: they know how much Mom loves them — her plush sons. Because however grown-up people become and however far the planes fly, every person in the world needs three things to the very last day: a happy childhood, a firm and reliable shore, and absolute, unshakable peace.

The Home's living, tailed guardians treat Butler with boundless respect. Alyonka (our Little Bun) is devoted and tender in her friendship with him, but has never allowed herself to tug at Butler's long ears. Alyonka knows the rules of etiquette: for rough-and-tumble play she has the Dachshund, while Butler is for the soul and for quiet. Even capricious Bibisechka, the cosmic girl, turns meek on his territory. She comes uninvited, gracefully steps around the Murano glasses, and curls up on the turquoise silk right beside the retriever, knowing that here no one will chase her away — and Butler will simply shield her from the night draft with his soft side.

Bear's life would be incomplete without this dog. For knowing everything in the world is a heavy cross to bear if there is no one to share a cup of night tea with, to sip a drop of memories from Murano glass with, and to keep silent about the most important things with. Butler guards this Home with his silence, and while the canvas bags wait for their hour by the door, absolute, unshakable peace reigns in the rooms.
