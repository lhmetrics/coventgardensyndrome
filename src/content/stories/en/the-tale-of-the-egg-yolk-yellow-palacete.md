## The Tale of the Egg-Yolk Yellow Palacete

There are Houses in my memory that no longer exist on this earth, but that have remained, forever, unbreakable fortresses in our hearts.

It was a beautiful Mansion... A true classic *Palacete*, built to last for centuries by a thorough, careful owner. We came across it, and its owners, during a long search for a place of our own. The house belonged to a lovely couple of very grown-up people, and the space was an exact reflection of who they were.

The Mansion took its guests into its embrace from the very threshold — from the soft crunch of fine gravel under the car's wheels. It opened itself up with the half-circle of its grand entrance, which seemed to invite you in to plunge headlong into bliss, peace and harmony.

Only people like them could have created such a world. The owners were kind and open, and their House and Garden were exactly the same. A bright façade the color of egg yolk, snow-white balustrades, columns and strict cornices — it all looked festive and dressed for an occasion against the dense green of the trees.

Inside reigned a celebration of sunlight and space. The heart of the house was a room for a real grand piano, its windows looking out onto the well-kept garden. The garden was so alive that a cockatoo had settled in it — evidently one that had flown away from its former owners and chosen freedom among the flowers. And under the roof, year after year, swallows built their nests without fear. A fairy tale? No — it was our reality, and we have remembered it forever.

The master of the house was a tough nut in business, yet he had stayed a mischievous boy at heart, with a weakness for pranks and good drinks. His pride and passion was a massive bar, carved from oak, standing right in the living room. His wife would sometimes scold him for this fondness, tenderly and with a touch of pretense, but it didn't trouble him in the least — he would only smile good-naturedly in reply.

The lady of the House was a true lady. Her great passion was collecting and studying historic lace. That is exactly what our friendship grew from, despite the language barrier. They preferred their native language to all others, knowing only a couple of stock phrases and the simplest words in any other tongue. At their stage of life, foreign languages were simply no longer needed. This is how people live who have found complete harmony: in their settled, sheltered world there is simply no room for outside sounds and meanings. We didn't know their language then, and we still don't — but does a synergy of souls really need words?

We loved visiting each other for tea and sweets, sharing our national dishes and jars of homemade jam. We simply contemplated beauty, sorted through antique lace and talked about the little things of everyday life.

While we women cooked together in the comfortable, beautiful kitchen, our hosts kindly handed the grand piano over to our younger son. And to the sound of a child's music we carried the treats into the living room, where the men were already waiting for us at their oak bar, unhurriedly swirling rocks glasses of amber whisky.

The mansion also had a spacious dining room. Its walls the lady had covered in fabric with her beloved paisley pattern, and deep in the room there hid a secret door that a stranger's eye would never have found. It was a sheltered world in which their children and grandson had grown up — for him, a nursery with a collection of Lego was carefully kept in the house. He was always awaited there. It was a House full of harmony and love.

...In its place there now stands a strange modern structure of picket fencing, like a faded shed somewhere deep in the woods. The cockatoo has no home anymore; there is no flowering garden, no swallows under the roof. There is only a flat stretch of earth and a gray wooden barrack. That is how swiftly and mercilessly the world around us has changed.

But in my memory this sunny, egg-yolk yellow House and its people are eternal. And this keepsake of a warmth now gone, no one can ever take from us.
