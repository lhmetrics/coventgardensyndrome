## What Things Stay Silent About (In Place of a Foreword)

"Every house knows how to speak about its owners. In the psychology of space there's a striking and very precise term — Environmental Self-Reflection, meaning the reflection of one's own 'Self' through interior design. If you pause for a moment and simply spend time in a person's personal space, you can understand their character, tastes, and even their thoughts far more deeply than after long conversations.

We don't fill our beloved Home with objects by chance. We intuitively choose what resonates within us, what is able to warm the walls and reflect our inner world. This is how things become an extension of our personality and keepers of the home's warmth.

But antique objects also have their own secret life, one that went on long before us. A master's hallmark on a silver spoon, a barely visible engraving on a clock from Covent Garden, a signature on the back of a yellowed painting — these are ciphers left behind by time. And deciphering them turns out to be not just fascinating, but remarkably useful.

In this section, we'll turn into researchers. We'll carefully clear the dust of years from antique hallmarks, reconstruct chronologies, search for the names of the craftsmen, and unravel the mysteries that these objects brought with them into our Home. Because by knowing an object's past, we truly make it part of our present."
